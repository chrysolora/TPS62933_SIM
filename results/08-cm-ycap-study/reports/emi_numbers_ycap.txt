--- comparison: series CM choke, first-order (C_p as Norton impedance) ---
   NOTE: the baseline uses an IDEAL CM current source (Z_Cp = inf).  A series
   choke only reduces the current if C_p is given its Norton impedance
   Z_Cp = 1/(2*pi*f*C_p):  I_eff = I_cm * |Z_Cp/(Z_Cp + Z_choke)|  (the small
   ~25 ohm loop impedance is neglected).  Z_choke = 2*pi*f*L (ideal).
   1 mH     choke  noload=-22.14  half=-28.99  full=-27.74
   4.7 mH   choke  noload=-13.62  half=-25.38  full=-24.13
   10 mH    choke  noload= -8.72  half=-20.49  full=-19.24
   choke effectiveness vs frequency (1 mH): reduction of I_cm in dB:
      f=  0.81 MHz : Z_Cp=  19770.8 ohm  Z_choke=   5058.0 ohm  ->  -0.28 dB
      f=  2.00 MHz : Z_Cp=   7957.7 ohm  Z_choke=  12566.4 ohm  ->  -5.43 dB
      f= 10.00 MHz : Z_Cp=   1591.5 ohm  Z_choke=  62831.9 ohm  -> -31.93 dB
      f= 30.00 MHz : Z_Cp=    530.5 ohm  Z_choke= 188495.6 ohm  -> -51.01 dB
   NOTE: a CM choke is SERIES impedance, and it competes against Z_Cp.  At
   0.805 MHz Z_Cp is high (~20 kohm) vs a 1 mH choke (~5 kohm) -> tiny effect;
   it only bites at high frequency where Z_Cp has fallen.  Opposite trend to
   the shunt Y-cap.  A real choke also self-resonates (parasitic C) so the
   >10 MHz figures are optimistic.
